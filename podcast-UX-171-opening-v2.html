OMTM and North Star Metrics Without Tunnel Vision

This recording is an editorial draft adapted from the revised lesson. It has not completed the final dialogue and audio review.

Before we begin

Host: OMTM and North Star Metrics Without Tunnel Vision.

Guide: The OMTM is stage- and problem-specific. It asks: what single measure best tells this team whether it is overcoming the constraint that matters now? During problem discovery it might be evidence of need; during activation it might be meaningful first success; during retention it might be repeated value. It should change when the constraint changes.

OMTM and North Star serve different horizons

Host: What changes when we look at omtm and north star serve different horizons?

Guide: A North Star metric is more durable. It represents the recurring event or result in which customers receive core value and the product creates sustainable value. It is not simply revenue, users, clicks, or transactions. A useful North Star has a clear value event, qualifying conditions, actor, frequency, and connection to strategy.

Guide: Focus measures sit inside an architecture. Input metrics reveal levers; diagnostics explain movement; guardrails protect quality and stakeholders; outcomes validate whether the focus still predicts value.

Test a candidate focus metric

Host: What changes when we look at test a candidate focus metric?

Guide: Ask:

Guide: Does the unit represent delivered value rather than activity or availability? Can teams influence it through legitimate product and service improvements? Is it sensitive enough to guide current decisions? Does it have a plausible relationship to durable user and organisational value? Can it be defined consistently and audited? How can it be gamed?

Guide: Which people, contexts, or sides of the system can it sacrifice? What metric should replace it when the constraint changes?

Guide: Use a “perverse design” exercise: deliberately invent ways to increase the metric while harming users. Those examples expose missing qualifying conditions and guardrails.

Transfer example: language learning

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: language learning?

Guide: “Lessons completed” can be increased with shorter lessons and pressure notifications. “Learners who successfully practise and return at an appropriate cadence” is closer to recurring value, but still needs comprehension, frustration, accessibility, and notification guardrails.

Guide: During early activation work, the OMTM might be target learners reaching one meaningful first success; later it may shift to sustained practice.

Multi-sided systems need balance

Host: What changes when we look at multi-sided systems need balance?

Guide: Marketplaces, care services, and platforms create value through several actors. Orders can grow while suppliers are overwhelmed; patient bookings can rise while clinic rejection climbs. Avoid averaging unlike value into an opaque composite. Use a clear focus measure with participant-balance diagnostics and veto guardrails.

Guide: If one side’s capacity is the constraint, the OMTM may intentionally focus there for a period.

Where this can go wrong

Host: What changes when we look at where this can go wrong?

Guide: Vanity North Star: a large cumulative total feels impressive but guides no value decision. Permanent OMTM: yesterday’s constraint governs today’s work. Metric monoculture: guardrails and qualitative evidence lose authority. Goodhart capture: teams learn to increase the number without the construct.

Guide: One-sided value: customers improve by extracting unsustainable labour from partners or staff. Average blindness: growth in one segment conceals exclusion elsewhere.

Define focus with an expiry and guardrails

Host: What changes when we look at define focus with an expiry and guardrails?

Guide: The team may temporarily focus on clinic acknowledgement for valid transfers because the service blueprint identifies an unowned handoff. The metric has an expiry condition: once reliability reaches the agreed level, focus shifts to the next constraint.

Guide: Outcome and guardrails remain visible throughout, including unsuitable acceptance, original-slot loss, unequal delay, and coordinator burden.

Guide: Targets use baseline, uncertainty, capacity, and expected mechanism. They are not motivational numbers chosen by leadership. Review asks why the metric moved and whether the behaviour it encouraged still serves the outcome. Teams can stop using an OMTM that has become a gaming target or no longer represents the bottleneck.

Transfer example: marketplace

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: marketplace?

Guide: Completed transactions can be a North Star only if they represent genuine buyer and seller value. Fraud, cancellation, dispute, delivery failure, and seller sustainability prevent the number from becoming a tunnel-vision target.

The harder decision

Host: Where does the harder design decision appear?

Guide: Focus is an allocation decision. Senior practitioners explain why this constraint matters now, what work it displaces, and when focus ends. They do not cascade the North Star into individual performance targets; that invites local gaming. They review the metric whenever teams discover a reliable way to move it without improving the underlying outcome.

Focus attention without collapsing value

Host: What changes when we look at focus attention without collapsing value?

Guide: The metric definition includes eligible population, quality threshold, time, and guardrails. The team identifies how it could be gamed—for example, accepting only easy cases—and reviews a balanced scorecard. Focus helps coordination only when the omitted dimensions remain protected.

Product example

Host: Can we see that in a concrete product situation?

Guide: Illustrative scenario: a marketplace. A north-star measure of completed, trusted transactions may align teams better than page views. But one number cannot represent seller fairness, dispute resolution, or people excluded before purchase. The team pairs its focus measure with guardrails and revisits it when incentives cause gaming.

Try it yourself

Host: What should I notice the next time I use a product?

Guide: Infer the North Star of a product from its interface and notifications. What behaviour is it encouraging? Name one stakeholder or quality the apparent metric could make invisible.
