Products are part of services

A conversation-first opening for this lesson. Alex and the guide work through the idea together, continuing the CarePath story.

Text preview only. This has not been recorded or added as an audio version. The written lesson remains below the player in the course.

Guide

Maya says her referral vanished. CarePath says it was sent. The clinic says it never arrived. Which screen would you redesign?

Alex

I was going to make the status clearer. But I don't know what happened to the referral.

Guide

Good catch. Today we'll follow a product promise through the people, systems, and rules that must keep it. That's service design.

Alex

All right. The doctor creates the referral. Then?

Guide

Secure transport sends it to an intake queue. Staff check eligibility and completeness. A clinician may triage it. Capacity determines the offer.

Alex

And the notification is at the very end. A lovely status page can't make those earlier steps happen.

Guide

Where would you draw Maya on the map?

Alex

Above the line: what she does and waits through. On the line: the app, messages, and conversations she can see. Below it: queues, staff, policies, and data moving between systems.

Guide

Follow the referral itself. Where does it change state?

Alex

Wait. The intake system calls it returned, but CarePath only knows pending. No one recorded why it came back.

Guide

Now why is the message vague?

Alex

Because the service doesn't have a shared answer. Better copy alone would pretend to know more than it does.

Guide

What needs to change besides the page?

Alex

Shared referral states, a reason for exceptions, staff access to the missing information, a real time expectation for Maya, and someone responsible when a referral stalls.

Guide

Do you own every operational process now?

Alex

No. The blueprint makes the dependencies visible so clinical, operations, policy, and engineering can decide together.

Guide

Exactly. A customer sees one experience even when our organisation sees separate teams. Next we'll test whether a proposed improvement helps that whole experience.
