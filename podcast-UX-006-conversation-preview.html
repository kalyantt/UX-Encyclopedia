The UX quality lens

A conversation-first opening for this lesson. Alex and the guide work through the idea together, continuing the CarePath story.

This conversation has a short recorded preview in the lesson player. It is not the full episode. The written lesson remains in the course.

Alex

The team wants everyone to create an account before seeing appointments. They say it will make the journey easier to track.

Guide

For whom?

Alex

For the organisation, certainly. It could also help patients manage requests. But I haven't checked what it costs to get in.

Guide

That's our question today. A promising metric can show one kind of success while concealing another kind of failure. We'll use six lenses to decide what quality means here.

Alex

First: outcome. Are more people reaching appropriate care, not just finishing registration?

Guide

Yes. Usability?

Alex

Can they get through identity checks, recover a password, and fix a mismatch without losing the request?

Guide

Context?

Alex

Someone may need care urgently, have weak connectivity, or not control an email address.

Guide

Inclusion?

Alex

Test assistive technology, language, digital confidence, and an equivalent route to a person who can help.

Guide

Agency and trust?

Alex

Explain why identity is needed and what data is collected. Don't require an account just to learn whether the service can help.

Guide

And service value?

Alex

Can staff keep the records accurate? What happens to support demand, duplicate identities, and maintenance?

Guide

So, ban accounts?

Alex

No. Let people browse services and broad availability first. Ask for identity when they submit or manage a request, explain why, and keep a phone route.

Guide

How will you know that choice holds up?

Alex

Measure access to care, not only sign-ups. Watch abandonment, account recovery, support calls, identity errors, and differences across groups. Record who owns the trade-off and when we review it.

Guide

That's the foundation we've built: the experience brief, usability scorecard, experience goals, context scenarios, service blueprint, and quality decision log. Next we'll look at human perception, which complicates every one of them.
