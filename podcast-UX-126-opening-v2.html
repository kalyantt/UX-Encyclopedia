Make Evidence Actionable for Different Audiences

This recording is an editorial draft adapted from the revised lesson. It has not completed the final dialogue and audio review.

Before we begin

Host: Make Evidence Actionable for Different Audiences.

Guide: Research communication is a design activity. Start with the decision and audience, then select the evidence, detail, artifact, and recommendation that lets that audience act. A journey fragment may help an operations lead; an annotated prototype may help engineering; a concise opportunity and outcome may help leadership.

Why it happens

Host: Why does that happen?

Guide: Do not tailor by hiding uncertainty. Make the claim, evidence, confidence, implication, trade-off, and next learning step clear. The aim is informed action, not persuasion at any cost.

Change the resolution, not the truth

Host: What changes when we look at change the resolution, not the truth?

Guide: Every communication names the decision, evidence, limits, recommendation, owner, and next review. Clips and stories illuminate patterns while traceable records support challenge. The team observes what audiences do after the communication—what decision changed, what question remained, and whether evidence was misinterpreted—then improves the format.

Design evidence as a service

Host: What changes when we look at design evidence as a service?

Guide: Map audiences by decisions, authority, prior knowledge, cadence, and required detail. Executives may need outcome, risk, and strategic choice; product teams need opportunity and evidence; designers and engineers need scenarios, states, and constraints; operations need volume, handoff, and ownership. Tailor entry points without changing claims.

Guide: Create layers: concise decision summary, visual model, key evidence and counterevidence, implications, and traceable source repository. Use shared terminology and date or version. Separate current finding from recommendation so audiences can challenge the inference.

Guide: Place evidence in existing decisions—planning, critique, prioritisation, design review, release, and incident review—rather than relying on a final presentation. Assign owners and next actions, then track whether findings changed work and outcomes.

Guide: Maintain accessibility, confidentiality, and permissions. Record limitations and expiry; evidence from an old market or product version should not circulate as timeless truth. Actionability is a property of timing and ownership as much as storytelling.

Transfer example: fraud research

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: fraud research?

Guide: A generic report on customer confusion will not change a fraud rule. Risk leaders need false-positive consequence and control options; support needs recovery patterns; designers need comprehension and state requirements. The same evidence is translated to each decision without softening uncertainty or hiding affected customers.

The harder decision

Host: Where does the harder design decision appear?

Guide: If a presentation ends with “any questions?” but no owner, decision, or next test, it communicated information without enabling action.

Look more closely

Host: What should we look at more closely?

Guide: Create an evidence handoff that preserves the decisions other disciplines need to make. Engineers may need acceptance conditions, states, data, and technical risk; content designers may need language, meaning, and timing; operations may need capacity, handoffs, and failure cases; leaders may need outcome, confidence, trade-off, investment, and accountability.

Guide: Keep the underlying evidence available so tailored communication does not turn into distortion.

Guide: Failure modes to watch

Guide: One-size readout: the same generic report produces no specific next action. Executive simplification: uncertainty and affected groups disappear in a summary. Research handoff: evidence arrives after the relevant decision is already fixed. Ownerless recommendation: a finding has no decision maker, deadline, or next learning step.

Product example

Host: Can we see that in a concrete product situation?

Guide: The same finding reaches a designer, engineer, executive, and support lead. Each needs a different next action: interaction change, feasibility constraint, investment decision, and service preparation. One generic slide deck serves none of them well.

Connect the lessons

Host: How does this connect to what we have already learned?

Guide: M06 completes the evidence-to-decision chain. M07 applies it to information systems: the architecture of content, labels, navigation, and search.

Try it yourself

Host: What should I notice the next time I use a product?

Guide: Rewrite one insight for an engineer, a senior leader, and a support team. Keep the evidence constant; change only the decision-relevant framing.
