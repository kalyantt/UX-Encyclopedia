Diverge, Then Converge With Evidence

This recording is an editorial draft adapted from the revised lesson. It has not completed the final dialogue and audio review.

Before we begin

Host: Diverge, Then Converge With Evidence.

Guide: Divergence is valuable when it changes assumptions, mechanisms, or system boundaries. It can reopen the problem frame as well as the solution. Teams should diverge at four levels:

Divergence changes the decision space

Host: What changes when we look at divergence changes the decision space?

Guide: explanations: competing accounts of why the current outcome occurs; opportunities: different moments or actors where intervention could matter; mechanisms: prevention, information, capability, incentive, policy, automation, human judgement, or recovery; representations: alternative ways to enact and test promising mechanisms.

Guide: Convergence is not voting. It is a sequence of evidence-backed commitments under constraint. Some options should be eliminated by a veto condition such as unsafe delegation of clinical judgement. Some need a cheap probe. Some can coexist as a portfolio. Others should remain documented but dormant until an assumption changes.

Build an option portfolio

Host: What changes when we look at build an option portfolio?

Guide: Create one option card per materially different direction. Include the problem explanation, target outcome, mechanism, affected actors, value created, value displaced, dependencies, riskiest assumption, evidence already available, consequence of being wrong, and cheapest discriminating test.

Guide: Then use three passes:

Guide: 1. Eligibility: remove options that violate non-negotiable safety, accessibility, legal, ethical, or strategic constraints. Challenge habits disguised as constraints. 2. Discrimination: ask what evidence would distinguish the surviving options. Do not fully prototype every one. Test the assumptions most likely to reverse the decision. 3.

Guide: Commitment: select a direction, combination, or staged sequence. Record why, residual uncertainty, review triggers, and what would reopen divergence.

Guide: A weighted matrix can structure discussion but cannot make values objective. Keep vetoes, evidence confidence, and distributional effects outside the averaged score. A design that helps most patients while delaying urgent referrals for a smaller group is not adequately represented by a higher mean score.

Transfer example: failed delivery

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: failed delivery?

Guide: An ecommerce team might improve address entry, allow landmarks, predict delivery windows, enable courier contact, add pickup points, or change the failed-delivery policy. These options intervene at different causes and moments. Field observation can test address and coordination assumptions; an operations pilot can test pickup capacity.

Guide: The decision is a staged system, not the winning screen in a workshop.

Where this can go wrong

Host: What changes when we look at where this can go wrong?

Guide: Cosmetic portfolio: interface variants masquerade as strategic alternatives. Evidence asymmetry: the familiar option has data while novel options are rejected for uncertainty. Premature feasibility: current architecture becomes an unquestionable boundary. Endless optionality: no decision owner or convergence date exists.

Guide: Matrix laundering: contested values disappear inside scores. Sunk-cost defence: evidence changes but the chosen direction remains protected.

Create options at different system levels

Host: What changes when we look at create options at different system levels?

Guide: During divergence, ideas are recorded with the opportunity addressed, mechanism, assumptions, and likely harms. Feasibility questions are captured so they are not lost, but they do not let the present system define the entire solution space. Individual generation precedes group sharing to reduce anchoring and hierarchy.

Guide: Analogies are translated into principles rather than copied as features.

Guide: Convergence occurs in stages. The team first removes options that violate safety or ethics, then compares the rest against patient progress, clinical suitability, continuity, equity, staff capacity, technical feasibility, reversibility, and learning value. Evidence supports the criteria; voting only reveals preference.

Guide: Hybrid options are allowed when mechanisms reinforce each other.

Transfer example: reducing failed delivery

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: reducing failed delivery?

Guide: Options can change address data, courier navigation, recipient communication, building access, pickup policy, or merchant packaging. Diverging only on notification design hides most of the service system. Convergence should compare where each option changes the failure mechanism.

The harder decision

Host: Where does the harder design decision appear?

Guide: Senior practitioners make optionality affordable. They fund small probes before large commitments, sequence reversible decisions first, and identify one-way doors. They also inspect political dynamics: whose option receives research, whose constraints are believed, and whose labour is displaced.

Guide: The record should allow a future team to understand not only what was chosen but which changed condition would make a rejected option relevant again.

Separate expansion from commitment

Host: What changes when we look at separate expansion from commitment?

Guide: Convergence uses visible criteria derived from outcome and risk: patient progress, clinical safety, equity, capacity, evidence quality, reversibility, and learning value. Ideas can combine; attractive concepts can be rejected. The output is a portfolio of assumptions and next tests, not a voting result disguised as strategy.

Product example

Host: Can we see that in a concrete product situation?

Guide: Illustrative scenario: a team inbox. Complaints about missed messages lead to several ideas: stronger alerts, clearer ownership, a shared triage queue, and fewer notification types. Sketch them before choosing. Then test which approach helps teams assign and resolve real messages without creating a new interruption burden.

Guide: Divergence is useful only when convergence uses evidence.

Try it yourself

Host: What should I notice the next time I use a product?

Guide: Find a product that solves a problem mainly through interface friction. List one policy, infrastructure, human-service, and prevention alternative. What organisational assumption made the interface solution appear inevitable?
