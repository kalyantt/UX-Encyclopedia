Quantitative Evidence, Segments, and Triangulation

This recording is an editorial draft adapted from the revised lesson. It has not completed the final dialogue and audio review.

Before we begin

Guide: Today we're talking about Quantitative Evidence, Segments, and Triangulation. By the end, you'll know why this matters, how to use it, and what to notice in a real product.

Host: What is the problem we are trying to understand?

Guide: Quantitative evidence describes magnitude, distribution, sequence, and change. It is valuable for locating patterns, comparing segments, monitoring outcomes, and checking whether a problem is widespread. It does not automatically explain motivation or establish cause. Define the event, denominator, time window, and segment before interpreting a chart.

Triangulate disagreement

Host: What changes when we look at triangulate disagreement?

Guide: Suppose analytics shows many comparison views but few requests. Usability sessions show clear comprehension, while interviews report distrust of unfamiliar clinics. Operations data reveals several comparison options are stale. These findings are not averaged. They describe different parts of the mechanism: interface understanding, perceived risk, and data quality.

Guide: The next decision addresses freshness and trust information before redesigning the control. A controlled rollout may later estimate whether the complete intervention changes successful access, with guardrails for delay, error, staff load, and equity. Causal language is reserved for the design and conditions that warrant it.

Preserve denominators and competing explanations

Host: What changes when we look at preserve denominators and competing explanations?

Guide: Triangulation asks how sources with different weaknesses support or challenge an explanation. Queue logs can show delay, interviews show its effect and coping behaviour, observation reveals the handoff, and experiments may test an intervention. Disagreement is diagnostic: it can expose measurement error, selection, or genuine variation.

Failure modes in practice

Host: What changes when we look at failure modes in practice?

Guide: Convenient denominator: only successful entrants count, hiding access failure. Event faith: instrumentation is analysed before being reconciled with real system state. Metric substitution: clicks replace appropriate care, recovery, or human outcome. Segment fishing: enough cuts are explored to manufacture a story.

Guide: Triangulation by vote: conflicting sources are averaged rather than explained through their scope and blind spots.

Guide: Triangulation compares evidence from different methods or sources. Agreement can increase confidence; disagreement can expose a segment, measurement flaw, or mistaken assumption. Do not average conflicting evidence into a vague conclusion. Ask which method is credible for which claim and what next study could resolve the difference.

Make the population and denominator explicit

Host: What changes when we look at make the population and denominator explicit?

Guide: Define eligibility, exposure, unit, event, period, and outcome. Inspect instrumentation, missingness, duplicate events, selection, and survivorship before drawing a chart. Report absolute counts and rates; relative change without baseline can exaggerate small effects.

Guide: Choose segments from product mechanism or equity question, not endless exploratory slicing. Compare behaviour, outcome, error, and recovery across role, context, tenure, device, or capability where relevant and ethically collected. Small groups need uncertainty and privacy protection; absence of significance does not prove equality.

Guide: Use quantitative evidence to estimate pattern and qualitative evidence to explain mechanisms, meanings, and unseen populations. Operational records and support add system context. When sources disagree, investigate definitions, time, and selection rather than averaging.

Guide: Connect analysis to a decision and causal limits. Descriptive data shows what occurred under measurement; it does not prove an interface caused it. Define the next evidence capable of separating competing explanations.

Establish measurement validity before comparison

Host: What changes when we look at establish measurement validity before comparison?

Guide: Segmentation is based on a plausible mechanism and enough evidence, not post-hoc curiosity. The team examines route, clinic operating model, language, access condition, and referral complexity where these could change experience. It reports counts, uncertainty, and missingness and avoids making sensitive identity a proxy for an unmeasured structural cause.

Guide: Triangulation aligns time, population, and definition. If logs show faster acknowledgement but diaries show continued anxiety, perhaps the status does not communicate ownership or a subgroup experiences the long tail. The team uses qualitative evidence to interpret the metric and quantitative evidence to bound the pattern, then specifies what remains unobserved.

Transfer example: checkout conversion

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: checkout conversion?

Guide: A higher purchase rate among signed-in customers does not prove sign-in causes conversion; returning intent, stored payment, and device differ. A guest-checkout experiment, behavioural segments, usability evidence, and fulfilment outcomes answer different pieces of the decision.

The harder decision

Host: Where does the harder design decision appear?

Guide: Segmentation can illuminate different mechanisms or manufacture a story from noise. Define groups before inspecting outcomes, retain denominators and uncertainty, and involve affected expertise when categories carry social meaning. Triangulation should expose disagreement, not average it away.

Product example

Host: Can we see that in a concrete product situation?

Guide: Illustrative scenario: a job-search site. A survey says most respondents find filters useful, while logs show low use. Segment by task and experience level, check who answered the survey, and observe whether filters are discoverable. The sources need not be averaged into one score; their disagreement may reveal a more useful question.

Try it yourself

Host: What should I notice the next time I use a product?

Guide: Inspect a dashboard broken into segments. Ask who was eligible, who generated an event, who is missing, whether definitions differ across groups, and what qualitative evidence would explain the observed difference.
