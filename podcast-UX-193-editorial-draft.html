RITE for AI Interactions

This recording is an editorial draft adapted from the revised lesson. It has not completed the final dialogue and audio review.

Before we begin

Guide: Today we're talking about RITE for AI Interactions. By the end, you'll know why this matters, how to use it, and what to notice in a real product.

Host: What is the problem we are trying to understand?

Guide: Rapid Iterative Testing and Evaluation changes a design between sessions when an observed usability problem is sufficiently clear and the proposed correction is understood. The cycle is:

RITE is disciplined formative iteration

Host: What changes when we look at rite is disciplined formative iteration?

Guide: 1. observe behaviour in a meaningful task; 2. identify the problem and its consequence; 3. judge the strength and urgency of evidence; 4. form a specific explanation for why it happened; 5. make the smallest plausible correction; 6. predict what should change; 7. test the revision with the next appropriate participant; 8.

Guide: record whether the problem disappeared, moved, or created another issue.

Guide: RITE is not informal design-by-reaction. It requires planned decision rules, traceable versions, and periodic synthesis. Its purpose is to improve a formative design quickly, not estimate prevalence, prove superiority, certify safety, or replace summative evaluation.

Decide in advance what can change

Host: What changes when we look at decide in advance what can change?

Guide: Define evidence thresholds before sessions:

Guide: Immediate change: severe harm risk, blocking defect, broken prototype, or unambiguous communication failure with a low-risk fix. Change after confirmation: a repeated behavioural problem with a plausible common cause. Record and defer: preference, ambiguous interpretation, segment-specific need, or issue whose correction could alter study validity or create wider risk.

Guide: Stop the study: unexpected harm, privacy or consent failure, invalid simulation, or system behaviour outside approved safeguards.

Guide: One observation can be enough when consequence is high and the mechanism is clear. Several observations may be inadequate when the proposed cause is speculative. Involve the whole observation team in classification; a facilitator should not redesign alone during a break.

Freeze the changing AI system

Host: What changes when we look at freeze the changing ai system?

Guide: In a conventional clickable prototype, the same action usually produces the same state. AI behaviour can vary by model version, system instruction, prompt history, retrieval result, generation setting, tool state, safety filter, or upstream data.

Guide: Without a run record, the team cannot tell whether a participant struggled because of the interface, the output, or a hidden system change.

Guide: For every session preserve:

Guide: model/provider and version or snapshot where available; system and task instructions; retrieval corpus, document versions, and retrieved passages; user input and relevant conversation state; generation parameters and safety configuration; tools, permissions, external states, and failures; output shown, interface version, and human simulation; participant context, scenario, observation, and outcome.

Guide: Use curated output sets where the research question concerns presentation, mental model, or recovery. Use live model behaviour when variability itself is under study, with safeguards and logs. Do not mix the two and call the sessions equivalent.

Keep an iteration decision log

Host: What changes when we look at keep an iteration decision log?

Guide: Each change records:

Guide: observation and direct evidence; severity, frequency so far, and affected people; hypothesised mechanism; exact interface, prompt, data, or workflow change; expected behavioural effect; conditions that remain fixed; new risk or regression introduced; next scenario and participant needed; result and decision to retain, revise, or revert.

Guide: Retain previous versions. After several sessions, review changes together. Local fixes can accumulate into inconsistency, hide a deeper system problem, or optimise for the narrow sample.

Include failures deliberately

Host: What changes when we look at include failures deliberately?

Guide: Do not let rapid cycles polish only the happy path. Rotate planned cases:

Guide: ambiguous input and clarification; stale, missing, or conflicting retrieval; plausible confident error; refusal or abstention; latency and unavailable dependency; wrong permission or attempted prohibited action; partial external action; human disagreement and escalation; inaccessible interaction and alternate channel.

Guide: Observe whether people detect the condition, understand its consequence, preserve control, recover, and reach the service outcome. Fixing an error message can expose a deeper failure—for example, the alternate route does not have capacity.

Know when RITE cannot answer the question

Host: What changes when we look at know when rite cannot answer the question?

Guide: Pause rapid iteration when asking:

Guide: Which design performs better? How common is this problem? Does performance differ by group? Is the system fair, calibrated, safe, or robust? Does an intervention improve real outcomes? Is a model change better than the baseline?

Guide: These require stable conditions, appropriate samples, technical and field evaluation, controlled comparison, or longer observation. RITE can prepare the design for those studies; it cannot substitute for them.

Transfer example: support drafting

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: support drafting?

Guide: An agent cannot see which sentence in a draft comes from policy. After one clear failure, a team adds claim-level source highlighting and tests again. That is a good rapid iteration. When agents disagree about whether source highlighting improves resolution time, the team freezes the design and runs a stable workflow comparison rather than continuing to tune around anecdotes.

Where this can go wrong

Host: What changes when we look at where this can go wrong?

Guide: Change after every opinion: preference is treated as a usability defect. Unlogged model variation: different outputs are mistaken for design improvement. Moving-everything problem: prompt, model, interface, source, and task change together. Happy-path polishing: error, permission, latency, and recovery remain untested.

Guide: Local optimisation: one participant group improves while another becomes less safe or included. Iteration as certification: formative success is used to claim fairness, safety, or outcome impact. Accumulated inconsistency: many small fixes produce an incoherent product. No stopping rule: research continues despite harm, invalid simulation, or unmanageable instability.

The harder decision

Host: Where does the harder design decision appear?

Guide: Senior practitioners establish change authority, severity rules, version infrastructure, and the boundary between formative iteration and evaluative claims. They ensure rapid cycles include edge conditions and underrepresented groups, and periodically step back from local issues to reassess the use case, delegation contract, and service system.

Guide: Speed is valuable only when the evidence chain remains intact. The team should be able to explain what changed, why, which conditions were held stable, what improved, and what remains unproven.

Product example

Host: Can we see that in a concrete product situation?

Guide: Illustrative scenario: an AI writing assistant. After each short usability session, the team makes a small change to how suggestions are presented and tests again. RITE helps rapidly repair obvious interaction problems, but model variability and unstable outputs require careful records of prompts, versions, and test conditions.

Guide: Speed should not turn a narrow fix into a claim of overall reliability.

Try it yourself

Host: What should I notice the next time I use a product?

Guide: Compare two versions of an AI prototype or product release. Can you tell whether the model, prompt, data, interface, or workflow changed? Choose one observed problem and state the smallest correction plus the evidence needed to retain it. If every variable moves together, apparent improvement cannot be attributed or trusted.
