The context changes the design

A conversation-first opening for this lesson. Alex and the guide work through the idea together, continuing the CarePath story.

Text preview only. This has not been recorded or added as an audio version. The written lesson remains below the player in the course.

Alex

We said CarePath should help Maya feel oriented and in control. I tested the appointment offer at my desk. It works.

Guide

Where is Maya when she opens it?

Alex

On a train, between meetings. She has about ninety seconds. Ah. That's not my desk.

Guide

Today we'll test the same task in different contexts. The screen may stay the same while the conditions for success change.

Alex

Her connection drops just before she confirms. She comes back. Does the page tell her whether the first tap went through?

Guide

It doesn't. She might tap twice or give up. What does she need?

Alex

Saved state and an honest status. And a concise message she can understand at a glance.

Guide

Now George opens the same offer using magnification. He sees only part of the page at once.

Alex

If the meaning depends on two things being side by side, he misses it. We need structure that reflows, strong contrast, and labels that make sense without their position.

Guide

Aisha shares a family phone. Would you put the clinic name on the lock screen?

Alex

No. A privacy-safe notice, then a secure way to resume. Same appointment, different social risk.

Guide

And Priya, the receptionist, is answering calls while two systems show different referral states.

Alex

She needs to know where a status came from and which record she's changing. An interruption must not send her back to the wrong patient.

Guide

So what belongs in a context scenario?

Alex

Who is acting, their real goal, when and where it happens, device or assistive technology, time and privacy constraints, and what failure costs.

Guide

Are these four separate products?

Alex

No. We look for a flow robust across variation, with another route where needed. But a short response deadline is a policy problem; responsive CSS can't fix it.

Guide

Exactly. In the next lesson we'll follow the service machinery behind that visible moment.
