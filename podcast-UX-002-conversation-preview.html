Usability is not aesthetic taste

A conversation-first opening for this lesson. Alex and the guide work through the idea together, continuing the CarePath story.

Text preview only. This has not been recorded or added as an audio version. The written lesson remains below the player in the course.

Alex

Yesterday we asked whether Maya's whole attempt worked. Today the team wants me to choose between two booking screens. One is beautiful.

Guide

And the other?

Alex

Also beautiful. That's the problem. What am I comparing?

Guide

Try rescheduling Maya's appointment on each one. Tell me what happens.

Alex

On the first, I can check a new time before I give up the old one. On the second… wait, tapping the new slot cancels the original.

Guide

Would Maya know that before she tapped?

Alex

No. Both screens look calm. Only one lets her act safely.

Guide

That's our chapter: usability. We'll look at whether someone can reach the right result, how much work it takes, whether they understand it, and how they recover when things go wrong.

Alex

So not, ‘Which one do you like?’

Guide

Not as your first test. Set the task and context. Who is using it? What are they trying to do? What would count as a serious mistake?

Alex

Maya is changing an appointment on her phone between meetings. The right result is a slot she can attend, without losing the old one by accident.

Guide

Now watch, without helping her. Did she choose the right clinic and time? That's effectiveness.

Alex

How long she spent, what she reread, and whether she had to call for help: efficiency.

Guide

And if she chose wrongly?

Alex

Recovery. She needs a safe way back. I can't call a flow usable just because it was fast on the happy path.

Guide

Exactly. Ask what she believes happened, too.

Alex

She submitted the change. The screen says ‘Request received.’ If she thinks that means confirmed, our completion metric flatters us.

Guide

What would you change?

Alex

Show requested, under review, confirmed, or action required as different states. Name who acts next. Keep the information she's entered and let her change course.

Guide

Good. A plain screen that gets that right may be more usable than a polished one that hides the risk.

Alex

And next lesson we ask how that working journey should leave her feeling.
