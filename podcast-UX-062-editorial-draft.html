Generate Before You Judge

This recording is an editorial draft adapted from the revised lesson. It has not completed the final dialogue and audio review.

Before we begin

Guide: Today we're talking about Generate Before You Judge. By the end, you'll know why this matters, how to use it, and what to notice in a real product.

Host: What is the problem we are trying to understand?

Guide: Design requires two different modes of judgement. Divergence expands the set of problem interpretations and possible responses. Convergence reduces that set through evidence, criteria, and constraints. Mixing them too early causes every unfamiliar idea to be killed by the standards of the current system.

Divergence is variation, not decoration

Host: What changes when we look at divergence is variation, not decoration?

Guide: Generating five layouts for the same card is not meaningful divergence. The service model, information, timing, and responsibility remain unchanged.

Guide: Useful alternatives vary a mechanism: prevent the need, change who decides, reveal different information, move a decision earlier, offer human support, redesign a policy, change the channel, automate a bounded task, or make recovery safer.

Guide: Quantity can help because the first ideas are often conventional, but quantity is not the goal. The goal is a decision set that exposes assumptions and creates real strategic choice.

Separate generation from evaluation

Host: What changes when we look at separate generation from evaluation?

Guide: Before ideation, define a bounded opportunity using research:

Guide: the person and situation; the progress they are trying to make; evidence of the current breakdown; desired outcome; known constraints and non-negotiables; assumptions still open to challenge.

Guide: Generate independently before discussing. Silent sketching reduces anchoring on the most senior or quickest speaker. Use prompts that force structural variation: What if there were no screen? What if the service prevented the uncertainty? What if the choice belonged to the GP? What if information arrived earlier?

Guide: What if the patient had low vision, limited English, no smartphone, or an urgent condition? What would recovery look like?

Guide: Cluster ideas by mechanism, not appearance. Combine compatible mechanisms, but preserve genuine alternatives. Only then converge.

Guide: Agree criteria before advocates defend concepts. Useful criteria include user progress, inclusion, severity of being wrong, evidence strength, feasibility, clinical or legal risk, cost, reversibility, strategic fit, and time to learn. A scorecard makes reasoning visible, but arithmetic must not disguise value judgements.

Guide: A severe safety risk should not be averaged away by minor advantages.

Convergence is a commitment with an escape route

Host: What changes when we look at convergence is a commitment with an escape route?

Guide: Convergence should answer:

Guide: Which option or combination advances? Which evidence and criteria justify it? Which assumptions remain exposed? What result would cause reconsideration?

Guide: This avoids opposite errors. Endless divergence prevents delivery because every decision remains open. Premature convergence creates false momentum around the first plausible idea. Mature teams time-box exploration, make reversible commitments where possible, and preserve rationale so later evidence can change direction without restarting from memory.

Transfer example: checkout abandonment

Host: What changes when we look at transfer example: checkout abandonment?

Guide: A team might generate shorter forms, guest checkout, clearer delivery dates, saved carts, alternative payment, transparent duties, or a stock-reservation policy. These address different causal hypotheses. If the breakdown is late disclosure of international fees, a cleaner form is irrelevant. Diverging across mechanisms connects each solution to an explanation of the problem.

Where this can go wrong

Host: What changes when we look at where this can go wrong?

Guide: Cosmetic divergence: several layouts conceal one unchanged idea. Executive anchoring: a leader’s early preference narrows the room. Brainstorm theatre: a large wall of ideas has no path to decision. Constraint denial: concepts depend on impossible conditions. Taste convergence: “I like it” replaces outcome, scenario, and evidence.

Guide: Scorecard laundering: subjective weights appear as objective truth. Lost alternatives: rationale disappears and future teams repeat the work.

Create structural alternatives, not cosmetic variants

Host: What changes when we look at create structural alternatives, not cosmetic variants?

Guide: For the referral-verification problem, alternatives include a durable receipt, proactive reconciliation, clinic-side acknowledgement, a patient timeline, and a staffed exception route. Colour and layout variants of the same confirmation screen do not count as different concepts. Each alternative states its mechanism, dependencies, people helped, new burden, and failure mode.

The harder decision

Host: Where does the harder design decision appear?

Guide: Senior practitioners protect both modes. Create room for inconvenient interpretations, then make convergence accountable. Distinguish non-negotiable constraints—law, safety, accessibility—from habits presented as constraints. Bring operations and engineering in early enough to reveal system options, not merely approve interface concepts.

Product example

Host: Can we see that in a concrete product situation?

Guide: Illustrative scenario: a shared calendar. A team has three plausible ways to resolve scheduling conflicts: a side-by-side comparison, an automatic suggestion, and a negotiation prompt. Sketching all three before choosing exposes different assumptions about control and effort.

Guide: The team evaluates each against actual coordination tasks rather than polishing the first idea until alternatives become politically difficult.

Try it yourself

Host: What should I notice the next time I use a product?

Guide: Review a product feature announced as a solution. Infer the problem explanation behind it. Propose two materially different mechanisms for the same outcome. Notice whether the shipped feature represents genuine convergence or simply the first idea rendered convincingly.
