UX Value, Product Risk, and What Not to Digitise

A conversation-first opening for this lesson. Alex and the guide work through the idea together, continuing the CarePath story.

This conversation has a short recorded preview in the lesson player. It is not the full episode. The written lesson remains in the course.

Guide

Last time, evidence changed CarePath's plan. Now the team has three new proposals: mandatory digital booking, automatic symptom triage, and no phone support after launch.

Alex

That sounds efficient on a roadmap. Today we're asking whether any of it creates real value, and for whom?

Guide

Exactly. Start with the phone line. What happens if it disappears?

Alex

Straightforward requests may move faster. But someone in crisis, or without reliable access, loses the help that makes the task possible.

Guide

So whose cost did the saving remove?

Alex

It may have shifted work to patients and hidden failures from the product dashboard. Staff could still spend more time handling exceptions elsewhere.

Guide

How would you assess the automatic triage idea?

Alex

The possible error is serious. A confident wrong answer could delay care. We'd need clinical evidence, oversight, a clear boundary on what it may do, and recourse.

Guide

Could technology help without taking that authority?

Alex

Yes. Show service criteria, broad availability, preparation, and referral status. Assist staff with structured matching, but keep unusual cases and clinical urgency with accountable people.

Guide

And mandatory accounts?

Alex

Let people understand the service before asking them to register. Require identity when it's actually needed to submit or manage a request.

Guide

Notice the choice isn't ‘build it’ or ‘do nothing.’

Alex

We could repair information, support human work, automate one bounded step, or change the service rule itself.

Guide

Which risk would you investigate first?

Alex

The one with high consequence and weak evidence. Not the harmless preference that's easiest to test.

Guide

Good. What does the first release do?

Alex

Improve discovery, referral visibility, preparation, and reuse of information. Keep phone support connected to the same status. Treat symptom triage as a research question, not a promise.

Guide

That's the end of our foundation: use digital tools where they make a worthwhile outcome more reachable, and keep human judgment where it protects that outcome.
